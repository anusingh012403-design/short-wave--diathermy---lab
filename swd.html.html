Aim

To study the working of Shortwave Diathermy.

Theory: Short Wave Diathermy

The term ‘diathermy’ means ‘through heating’, producing deep heating directly in the tissues of the body. Externally applied heat sources such as hot towels, infrared lamps and electric heating pads often cause discomfort and skin burns long before adequate heat reaches the deeper tissues.

With the diathermy technique, the subject’s body becomes a part of the electrical circuit, so the heat is produced within the body and is not transferred through the skin. Another advantage is that treatment can be controlled precisely. Careful placement of electrodes localises the heat to the region to be treated, and the amount of heat can be closely adjusted by the circuit parameters.

Heating is carried out by high-frequency alternating current, generally at 27.12 MHz (wavelength 11 m). Because the current alternates, it is possible to pass a much greater current through the tissues to produce direct heating, similar to any other electrical conductor.

Circuit diagram: oscillator and patient circuits

Circuit description

  • The machine has two main circuits: an oscillating circuit, which produces the high-frequency current, and a patient circuit, connected to it, through which the energy is transferred to the patient.
  • Transformer T1 (primary from the mains) is a step-up transformer that provides EHT for the triode anode. A second winding supplies heating current to the cathode.
  • The tank (resonance) circuit is coil AB in parallel with condenser C1. Positive feedback is generated by coil CD.
  • Coil EF and variable condenser C2 form the patient’s resonator circuit, coupled to oscillator coil AB.
  • The anode supply is about 4000 V. Conduction in a triode occurs during the positive half-cycle. When the supply is rectified, the oscillations are continuous and more power is available.
  • To tune the two circuits, an ammeter is placed in series. C2 is adjusted for a maximum reading; the needle swings either side of the tuned position.
  • The maximum power is 500 W. A thermal delay in the anode supply stops current until the valve filament reaches adequate temperature. The patient circuit is switched on, then current is increased steadily.
  • A mains filter in the primary suppresses interference produced by the unit.
  • Intensity control can be done by (i) anode voltage, (ii) filament current, or (iii) grid bias. The finest control is by grid bias, using a variable grid-leak resistance R1.

Applications

  • Deep heating of muscles and joints for pain relief
  • Relief of muscle spasm and stiffness
  • Sprains, strains and soft-tissue injuries (after the acute phase)
  • Chronic inflammatory conditions such as arthritis and bursitis
  • Back and neck pain

Procedure (from the practical manual)

  1. Connect the two condenser pads to the output socket. Place the electrodes around the patient’s body where the treatment is to be given.
  2. Connect the mains cable and switch the unit ON. The Power ON switch must glow.
  3. Set the timer for the desired period.

Safety precautions

  • Remove all metal objects, jewellery and metal implants from the treatment area.
  • Check that the pads make good contact and that the patient is not touching metal or the machine.
  • Never use diathermy over the chest of a patient with a pacemaker, or on a pregnant abdomen, without medical advice.
  • Start with a low intensity and increase slowly while asking the patient about comfort.
  • Switch OFF before adjusting pads or leads, and never touch the output circuit while it is running.

      Learning outcomes

      By the end of this experiment you should understand:

      1. Working principle of SWD

      High-frequency current (27.12 MHz) passes through the body, so heat is produced inside the tissue and is controlled by the electrodes and circuit parameters.

      2. Main components of the SWD machine

      Mains filter, step-up transformer T1, triode oscillator, tank circuit AB with C1, feedback coil CD, patient resonator EF with C2, ammeter, timer and intensity control.

      3. Oscillator and patient circuits

      The oscillator generates the high-frequency current. The patient circuit transfers it to the body. Tuning C2 matches the two circuits.

      4. Role of electrodes

      Pads placed on opposite sides of the treatment area make the body part of the circuit and focus the heat where it is needed.

      5. Frequency and wavelength

      27.12 MHz gives a wavelength of about 11 m. The frequency is fixed for diathermy use, and the resonance is found by tuning.

      6. Basic operation and safety

      Connect the mains, switch ON after the thermal delay, set the timer and intensity, tune to the maximum meter reading, and keep metal away from the treatment area.
